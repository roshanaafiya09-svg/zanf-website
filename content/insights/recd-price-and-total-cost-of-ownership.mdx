"What does a RECD cost for a 500 kVA set?" is the most common first question we
get, and the honest answer is that the device is the part of the number that
varies least.

This piece explains what actually moves the figure, so you can read two quotes
side by side and tell which one has understood your site.

## The four components of any RECD quote

**1. The device.** Sized to the rating and to the exhaust geometry. Within a
rating band, genuine type-approved hardware does not vary wildly between honest
suppliers. If one quote is dramatically cheaper here, check that what is being
offered is a type-approved device — the approval is what you are buying.

**2. Installation.** Mechanical erection, exhaust integration, supports,
insulation and cladding, electrical and OBD wiring. This is where quotes
genuinely diverge, because this is where sites genuinely differ.

**3. Testing and commissioning.** Measurement under load, OBD calibration,
documentation, demonstration, handover. A quote with no line for this has not
removed the cost — it has removed the deliverable.

**4. Ongoing.** Periodic inspection and catalyst health checks, usually under an
AMC.

## What moves the installation number

Rating matters less than people expect. These matter more:

- **Access.** A basement plant room with a 900 mm door is a different job from a
  ground-floor set with a roller shutter. Lifting and handling can dominate.
- **Exhaust route.** Length, number of bends, existing silencer position and how
  much re-routing the retrofit forces.
- **Existing back pressure.** If the current route is already close to the
  engine's permissible limit, the retrofit has to improve the duct work, not
  just add to it.
- **Height and structure.** Roof-level plant, or a device that needs new
  structural support, adds design and scaffolding.
- **Number of sets and sequencing.** Several sets in one room is cheaper per set
  in mobilisation and more expensive in sequencing, because standby capability
  has to be maintained throughout.
- **Outage window.** Work restricted to nights or a single weekend costs more
  than work that can run through a week.

Every one of those is established at survey. That is why a firm price before a
survey is either padded to cover the unknowns or is going to change.

## Where padding hides

Three things to look for:

**A device-only price with installation "to be advised".** The number you were
shown is not the number you will pay.

**"Commissioning included" with nothing specified.** Ask what will be measured
and what you will be handed. If the answer is not a list, it is not a
deliverable.

**Consumables in the running-cost table.** A Platino RECD has none — no reagent,
no filter element. If a quote projects consumable spend, ask what for.

## Cost of ownership over the life of the device

Buying is one number. Owning is four:

**Fuel and performance.** This is the big one, and it is usually a zero rather
than a saving. A correctly specified device adds no measurable back pressure, so
fuel consumption is unaffected. A badly installed one that pushes the engine
past its permissible back-pressure limit costs fuel every hour the set runs —
which, over a data centre's weekly test regime, is not a rounding error.

**Maintenance.** Periodic inspection and a ΔP check. There is no filter change
and no reagent. The realistic ongoing cost is the AMC visit.

**Downtime.** One to three days per set at installation, and effectively none
afterwards if the device is instrumented and trending is being watched.

**Risk.** The cost of not being able to evidence compliance when asked. It is
hard to put a figure on and easy to underestimate, and it is the reason the
documentation pack is worth paying for.

## The question worth asking instead

Rather than "what does a RECD cost", ask:

> *What will this specific site cost to bring into compliance, and what will I
> be holding at the end of it?*

That question can only be answered after someone has looked at your exhaust
line — which takes half a day, and which we would rather do before quoting than
discover afterwards.
