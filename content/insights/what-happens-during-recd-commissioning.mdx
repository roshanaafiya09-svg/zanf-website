Installation gets the attention. Commissioning is the part that decides whether
you can defend the installation afterwards — and it is the part most often
compressed into "we started it and it ran fine".

Here is what a commissioning day should actually look like.

## Before the day

Commissioning is not a first look at the job. By the time it starts:

- the device is erected on its designed supports;
- exhaust integration is complete and joints are leak-checked;
- insulation and cladding are back on across the whole new section;
- sensor taps are fitted either side of the device and wired to the OBD panel.

If any of those are outstanding, what follows is not commissioning. It is
finishing the installation with the client watching.

## Stage one — cold checks

Before the set is started: mounting and support inspected, cladding and
lagging checked for gaps and hot spots, sensor continuity verified, panel
powered and reading.

Nothing dramatic happens here. It exists so that nothing dramatic happens later.

## Stage two — first run and stabilisation

The set is started and allowed to reach a stable operating temperature. This
matters more for a catalytic device than for most plant: the FOC and CST only
do their work above a temperature window, so readings taken on a cold engine
describe a device that is not yet working.

Inlet and outlet temperatures are watched until they settle.

## Stage three — measurement under load

This is the core of the day. Three things are recorded, at each load step the
site can provide:

**Back pressure.** Measured across the installed device and compared against
the engine manufacturer's permissible limit. This is the number that answers
"has this cost us any performance?" — and it is answered with an instrument, not
an assurance.

**Differential pressure (ΔP).** The pressure drop across the substrate. On day
one this figure is not interesting in itself; what makes it valuable is that it
becomes the **baseline** every future inspection is compared against. A device
whose ΔP is drifting upward is a device telling you something months before a
performance complaint would.

**Inlet and outlet temperature.** Confirmation that the device is operating in
the window where the catalysts function.

If the site cannot provide full load, the record states which load steps were
achieved. Where a load bank is needed to demonstrate performance properly, that
is quoted openly beforehand — quietly skipping it and writing "satisfactory" is
how a compliance file ends up worthless.

## Stage four — OBD calibration

The panel your team will rely on for the next several years is calibrated now.
Sensors are verified against known references and the commissioning values are
written into the record as the reference baseline.

A panel that reads confidently and wrongly is worse than no panel.

## Stage five — deviations

Anything outside the design basis is identified, rectified and **re-tested**.
The deviation log — including the items that were closed out — goes into the
handover pack.

A commissioning record with no deviations on a complex retrofit is not a
compliment to the installer. It usually means nobody looked.

## Stage six — demonstration and briefing

The device is demonstrated to the site team under running conditions, and the
readings are explained in plain terms:

- what a normal ΔP looks like on *this* set;
- what a rising ΔP means and over what timescale it matters;
- what the temperatures should be once warm;
- at what point to pick up the phone.

Ten minutes here saves a support call every quarter.

## Stage seven — the pack, and a signature

The handover pack is assembled and handed over:

- test readings — back pressure, ΔP, temperatures, load steps;
- type-approval and product certificates for the installed device;
- installation photographs, before and after;
- as-installed sketch of the exhaust route and mounting;
- OBD calibration record;
- operating and maintenance notes;
- manufacturer warranty registration, in your name;
- a signed handover certificate.

It is signed by the commissioning engineer and your nominated site
representative. One copy stays with you.

## What "done" means

The job is finished when your team can run the plant and answer for it without
calling us. That is a deliberately high bar, and it is the reason the
documentation is assembled during the work rather than reconstructed from
memory a fortnight later.
